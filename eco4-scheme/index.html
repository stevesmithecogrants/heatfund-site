What ECO4 is — and who pays

ECO stands for the Energy Company Obligation. ECO4 is the fourth phase of the scheme. Instead of the government paying grants directly, the law places an obligation on larger energy suppliers to fund improvements that cut energy waste in the homes of people who need help most. Ofgem administers the scheme and sets the rules suppliers and installers must follow.

Because funding comes through that supplier obligation, you do not have to be a customer of a particular supplier to qualify. Eligibility is about your household, your income or benefits, and how energy-efficient your home is — not about who sends your energy bill.

ECO4 takes a whole-house approach. Rather than funding one measure in isolation, an installer surveys your home and designs a package that lifts its energy performance overall. That package might combine insulation with a heating upgrade, for example, so the home holds heat better and costs less to run.

Who qualifies for ECO4?

Most households qualify through one of two routes: the main benefits route, or ECO4 Flex through their local council. In both cases, the property itself also has to meet the scheme's energy-efficiency rules.

The property rules

  • Your home normally needs an EPC rating of D, E, F or G
  • Owner-occupiers qualify, and private or social tenants can qualify with their landlord's permission
  • The home must be in Great Britain — ECO4 covers England, Scotland and Wales
  • A survey must show that the planned measures will improve the home's energy performance

The income rules

On the main route, a member of the household must receive a qualifying means-tested benefit (see the benefits rules below). If nobody in the household receives one of those benefits, you may still qualify through ECO4 Flex — Route 1 covers households with a combined income under £31,000, and Routes 2 and 3 cover other circumstances explained further down this page.

Households receiving Child Benefit only can also qualify on the main route where the household income is below the threshold that applies to their household type. If you are unsure which route fits you, run the eligibility check — it asks about benefits and income and points you to the right route.

The benefits rules

On the main ECO4 route, someone in the household must receive at least one of the qualifying benefits. The qualifying benefits are the main means-tested benefits, including:

  • Universal Credit
  • Pension Credit — Guarantee Credit and Savings Credit
  • Income-based Jobseeker's Allowance (JSA)
  • Income-related Employment and Support Allowance (ESA)
  • Income Support
  • Housing Benefit
  • Child Tax Credit and Working Tax Credit
  • Child Benefit, where household income is below the threshold for your household type

A few practical points about the benefits route:

  • The person receiving the benefit normally needs to live at the address being improved.
  • You will be asked for evidence of the benefit — an award letter or similar — before work is approved. Have it ready to avoid delays, because ECO4 has a fixed end date.
  • Receiving a benefit does not on its own guarantee funding: the property still has to meet the EPC and survey rules above.
  • Funded improvements are not treated as income, so having ECO4 work done does not reduce your benefit payments.
  • If nobody in the household receives a qualifying benefit, do not assume you are out — check the ECO4 Flex routes below before giving up.

ECO4 Flex: qualifying without benefits

ECO4 Flex — full name Local Authority Flexible Eligibility, often shortened to LA Flex — lets your local council declare a household eligible even when nobody receives a qualifying benefit. Councils publish their own Statement of Intent setting out how they apply the Flex routes, so the detail varies by area, but the three routes follow the same national framework:

Route 1 — Low income

Households with a combined gross income under £31,000 a year can be declared eligible under Route 1, even if no one in the home receives a qualifying benefit.

You will normally need to show evidence of household income, such as payslips or bank statements, for the council or installer to verify.

Route 2 — Proxy targeting

Route 2 uses proxies — indicators that a household is likely to be in fuel poverty — instead of a straight income test.

A household normally needs to meet any two of the Route 2 proxies in its council's Statement of Intent. The proxies can include things such as the home being in an area of high deprivation, receiving certain free support such as free school meals, or being referred through an advice or support service. Check your own council's Statement of Intent for the exact list it uses.

Route 3 — Health conditions

Route 3 covers people whose health is made worse by living in a cold home. You may qualify if you have a condition in one of these groups:

  • Cardiovascular conditions
  • Respiratory conditions
  • Immunosuppressed conditions
  • Limited mobility

Route 3 normally needs a referral or supporting evidence from a health professional, such as your GP or another NHS professional involved in your care.

Under Flex, your council issues a declaration confirming eligibility before an installer can proceed. Not every council publishes a Statement of Intent or offers every route, so availability depends on where you live. The eligibility check will ask about income and health so it can point you towards Flex if the benefits route does not fit.

Do not leave it too late. A typical ECO4 journey — eligibility check, survey, council declaration where Flex applies, then installation — takes weeks, not days. To have measures installed by 31 December 2026, you need to start well before that date. If your home misses the ECO4 deadline, check the Warm Homes Plan and the other schemes on our government grants page instead.

What replaces ECO4? There is no ECO5

The government has not announced an ECO5, and ECO4 is the final phase of the Energy Company Obligation in this form. From January 2027, the successor is the Warm Homes Plan, the government's wider programme for upgrading homes, cutting heating costs and supporting low-carbon heating such as heat pumps.

Other help also continues separately — for example, the Boiler Upgrade Scheme offers grants towards heat pumps and biomass boilers outside ECO4. See our Boiler Upgrade Scheme guide and the full round-up on the government grants page.

Frequently asked questions

1. Is ECO4 really free?

For eligible households, ECO4 measures are normally fully funded through the supplier obligation, so you do not pay for the work itself. If an installer ever suggests a contribution towards part of a package, it should be explained and agreed in writing after the survey — never demanded upfront before your eligibility is confirmed.

2. Do I need to receive benefits to qualify for ECO4?

Not always. The main route is through a qualifying means-tested benefit, but ECO4 Flex gives councils another way in: Route 1 for household income under £31,000, Route 2 using fuel-poverty proxies, and Route 3 for certain health conditions affected by a cold home.

3. What EPC rating does my home need?

Your home normally needs an EPC rating of D, E, F or G. ECO4 is aimed at the least efficient homes. If you do not know your rating, the eligibility check can help — an EPC lookup forms part of the process, and a survey will confirm the position.

4. Can tenants get ECO4, or is it owners only?

Tenants can qualify as well as owner-occupiers, but the landlord's permission is needed for work on a rented property. If you rent, speak to your landlord early so permission does not hold up an application close to the deadline.

5. When does ECO4 end, and what happens if I miss it?

ECO4 ends on 31 December 2026, and measures must be installed by that date. There is no ECO5. If you miss ECO4, support continues through the Warm Homes Plan from January 2027, and separate schemes such as the Boiler Upgrade Scheme continue in their own right.

6. Can ECO4 replace my boiler on its own?

Usually not on its own. ECO4 follows a whole-house approach, so a boiler replacement is normally funded as part of a package that also improves the home's insulation or overall energy performance. What your home can get is confirmed at survey.

7. Will having ECO4 work done affect my benefits?

No. ECO4 funding is not counted as income for benefit purposes, so having measures installed does not reduce your benefit payments.
