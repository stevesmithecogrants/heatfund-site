How the maths works

  • System size: your annual electricity use (bill ÷ unit price) divided by about 850 kWh generated per kW each year, limited by your roof — about 6 m² per kW — and capped at 10 kW.
  • Panels: system size ÷ 465W per panel, rounded up. We install 465W panels.
  • Savings: each kWh generated is worth about 19p on average — the share you use at home (about 55%) saves your full unit price, and the rest (about 45%) is exported at about 12p per kWh.
  • Cost: about £1,650 per kW installed, before any grant or group-buying discount. Your written quotes set the real price.
  • Payback: estimated cost ÷ estimated yearly saving. Panels typically last 25 years or more, so savings can carry on well past payback.
  • 0% VAT: domestic solar installations are charged 0% VAT until 31 March 2027 — that saving is built into a proper installer quote.

What changes your number

Daytime use matters most. Electricity you use while the sun is shining is worth your full unit price; electricity you export is worth much less. If you are out all day, a battery can raise how much you use yourself — see our Battery Storage guide — but it adds cost, so it does not always shorten payback.

Roof direction and shade matter too. A south-facing roof with little shade generates the most. East–west roofs still work well, and a shaded roof may need a different layout or may not pay back. A proper survey settles this before you commit to anything.

Paying for solar: cash or finance

You can pay for a system outright, or spread the cost with finance. Finance is subject to a credit check and the lender's terms, and the rate depends on your circumstances — we do not set it, and no rate is promised here. What matters is the total amount repayable, not just the monthly figure, so compare that on any finance offer. If a grant or group-buying scheme could cut the cost first, check that before you sign anything — start with the free eligibility check.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is this calculator?

It is a guide, not a quote. It uses typical UK figures — about 850 kWh generated per kW each year, about 6 m² of roof per kW, and about £1,650 per kW installed. Your roof, your tariff and how much electricity you use during the day all move the real numbers. A survey and itemised quotes give you the exact figures for your home.

Why does roof space limit the system?

Each kW of panels needs roughly 6 m² of usable roof. A small roof simply cannot hold a big system, however much electricity you use. If your roof is the limit, the calculator sizes the system to fit it.

What if I use most electricity in the evening?

Then you use less of your own solar directly, and more of it is exported at a lower price — so savings are lower. A battery can help by storing daytime solar for the evening, but it adds cost. Our Battery Storage guide explains the trade-off.

Is there a free grant for solar panels?

There is no single universal free solar grant for every household. Help depends on the scheme, your area, your income or benefits, and your property. Some households may qualify through wider home-upgrade schemes — check the current routes in our Government Grants guide and start with a free eligibility check.

What does 0% VAT actually save me?

For a qualifying domestic installation, VAT is charged at 0% instead of the standard rate until 31 March 2027. On a £6,500 system that is a material difference versus a 20% rate — check the VAT line is shown as 0% on your quote. See our VAT Relief guide.

Should I add a battery at the same time?

Not always. A battery can help you use more of your own solar in the evening, but it adds cost. Many households start with panels, watch their usage for a few months, then decide. Our Battery Storage guide explains the trade-off.
