Insulation Grants · UK 2026

Free insulation grants UK 2026: save up to £590/year

A poorly insulated home loses heat through its roof, walls and floors all winter long. Government-backed schemes can pay for insulation in eligible homes — in some cases in full. This guide explains what is available, what each measure costs, and how to check whether your home may qualify.

Key Takeaways

  • Loft insulation can save a typical home around £590 a year on energy bills, and cavity wall insulation around £395 a year.
  • Good insulation can cut a home's heat loss by up to 45%.
  • The main live route is ECO4, which runs until 31 December 2026, alongside the Warm Homes: Local Grant in participating council areas.
  • The Great British Insulation Scheme is now closed to new applications.
  • Eligibility usually depends on your home's EPC rating, your heating type, and household income or benefits — a free check comes before any promise of funding.

Why insulation comes first

Heating a leaky home is money out of the roof. Around a quarter of a home's heat can escape through an uninsulated roof, and around a third through uninsulated walls. Adding insulation slows that loss down, so rooms stay warmer for longer and the boiler or heat pump does not have to work as hard.

That is why insulation is often the first step before other upgrades. A well-insulated home is also easier and cheaper to heat with a heat pump later, because the system can run at lower temperatures.

Up to 45% less heat loss: fitting a full package of suitable insulation measures can reduce how much heat escapes from a home by up to 45%, depending on the property and what is already installed.

Frequently asked questions

1. Is insulation really free under these schemes?

It can be. Where your home and household meet the rules of ECO4 or the Warm Homes: Local Grant, funding can cover the full cost of suitable measures. In other cases it may cover part of the cost. You should always be told the amount in writing before work starts.

2. How much can loft insulation save me?

Around £590 a year for a typical home moving from little or no loft insulation to the recommended depth. Homes that already have some insulation will save less from a top-up.

3. How much can cavity wall insulation save me?

Around £395 a year for a typical home. It only applies to homes built with cavity walls — a survey confirms whether your walls have a cavity and whether it is empty.

4. My walls are solid. Can I still get help?

Yes, solid wall insulation can be included, particularly under ECO4 as part of a wider package. It is the most expensive measure, which is exactly why grant funding matters most for it.

5. What EPC rating does my home need?

Most routes focus on homes rated D, E, F or G, because those homes benefit most. If you do not know your rating, the eligibility check can look at your property's EPC record.

6. Do I need to be on benefits to qualify?

Not always. Benefits are one route into ECO4, but councils can also declare households eligible under LA Flex based on income or health, and the Warm Homes: Local Grant uses an income test rather than benefits alone.

7. Is the Great British Insulation Scheme still open?

No. The Great British Insulation Scheme has closed to new applications. The live routes are ECO4 (until 31 December 2026) and the Warm Homes: Local Grant in participating areas.

8. When does ECO4 end?

ECO4 is scheduled to end on 31 December 2026. Because surveys, approvals and installation all take time, it is sensible to check well before that date rather than at the last minute.

9. Can tenants get insulation grants?

In some cases, yes — usually based on the tenant's circumstances, and always with the landlord's written permission. Landlords may also need to meet minimum energy efficiency rules for rented homes.

10. How long does installation take?

Loft and cavity wall insulation are often finished within a day. Underfloor and room-in-roof work can take one to two days, and solid wall insulation is a bigger job that can run over several days to a few weeks.

11. Will insulation make my home too warm in summer?

Insulation slows heat moving in both directions, so it helps keep heat out on hot days as well as keeping it in during winter. Normal ventilation still matters, and your installer should not block vents your home needs.

12. What happens after I check?

You are told whether you may qualify and what the next step is. If a survey is needed, that is arranged with you. Nothing is booked and no work goes ahead without your agreement and written confirmation of funding.
