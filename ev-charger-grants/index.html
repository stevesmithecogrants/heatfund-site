EV charger grants UK 2026: up to £500 off home charging

If you rent your home, own a flat, or have to park on the street, a government grant can take up to £500 off the cost of a home EV chargepoint. This guide explains who can claim, how much each route pays, what a charger actually costs, and how to apply before the schemes close.

Deadline: the home, landlord and workplace chargepoint grants are funded until 31 March 2027. The grant has to be applied for before the charger is installed, by an OZEV-authorised installer — so do not book the work first and ask about the grant afterwards.

Key takeaways

  • Up to £500 off: the EV chargepoint grant covers 75% of the cost of buying and installing a home charger, capped at £500 per socket.
  • Who it is for: renters, flat owners, and households whose only parking is on the street. Owner-occupiers of houses with a driveway are not covered by the home grant.
  • Separate routes exist for residential landlords and for workplaces — each also capped at up to £500 per socket under the current GOV.UK guidance.
  • Every chargepoint grant route ends on 31 March 2027. Apply before installation, through an OZEV-authorised installer.
  • Buying the car too? The separate Electric Car Grant takes £3,750 or £1,500 off eligible new electric cars, depending on the band the car falls into.
  • Typical cost: a smart 7kW home charger usually costs around £900–£1,300 installed before any grant.

Who can claim — and who cannot

You may qualify if…

You rent your home (with your landlord's written permission), you own and live in a flat (with freeholder or management-company consent where needed), or your home has no driveway and you rely on on-street parking with an approved cross-pavement charging solution. Your vehicle also needs to be on the OZEV eligible-vehicle list, and the work must be done by an OZEV-authorised installer using an approved smart chargepoint.

You usually cannot claim if…

You own the house you live in and it has its own driveway or garage — the old homecharge scheme for that group closed in 2022, and the current home grant does not cover it. You also cannot claim retrospectively: if the charger is already installed, or the work started before the application was approved, the grant will not be paid.

Renters and flat owners

This is the main home route. You need to live at the property, have suitable parking connected to it, and get permission from whoever controls the building or the land the cable crosses. For renters that means your landlord's agreement in writing; for flat owners it usually means the freeholder or management company. Get that permission first — installers will ask for it as part of the application, and sorting it early is the step that most often delays a claim.

Parking on the street

Until recently, no driveway meant no home charging. Cross-pavement solutions — most commonly a channel set into the pavement that your charging cable sits safely inside — have changed that. If your council supports this kind of installation and your home has suitable on-street parking, the chargepoint grant can contribute up to £500 (75% of eligible costs) towards the charger and the cross-pavement work. Check with your council as well as your installer, because pavement works need local approval.

Landlords and workplaces

Residential landlords can claim towards chargepoints at properties they let, which is becoming a genuine selling point for tenants who drive electric. Employers can use the Workplace Charging Scheme for staff and fleet charging. Both routes follow the same basic pattern: an OZEV-authorised installer, an application made before the work, and the grant taken off the cost once the claim is approved. Neither route is covered in depth here because this guide is written for households — if that is you, start with the check above and tell us you are a landlord or a workplace.

What a home EV charger costs

A standard smart 7kW home chargepoint typically costs around £900–£1,300 installed, before any grant. Straightforward jobs — a charger on the wall next to the consumer unit, with a short cable run — sit at the lower end. Longer cable runs, a separate garage, groundworks, or an older fuse board that needs upgrading push the price up. Always get the quote broken down, so you can see what the grant is actually being applied to.

Worked example, with the grant

Installation quoted at £1,000. The grant pays 75% of eligible costs capped at £500, so the full £500 cap applies. You pay £500. On a £600 job, 75% is £450 — the grant pays £450 and you pay £150.

Running cost at home

Charging overnight on an off-peak EV tariff, driving around 10,000 miles a year, typically works out at roughly £300 a year in electricity. Public rapid charging costs considerably more per mile, which is why most EV drivers do the bulk of their charging at home.

How to claim, step by step

  1. Get permission. Renters need their landlord's written agreement; flat owners may need freeholder or management-company consent; on-street households need a council-approved cross-pavement arrangement.
  2. Choose an OZEV-authorised installer and an approved smart chargepoint. Only authorised installers can submit the grant application.
  3. Apply before any work starts. Your installer submits the application with your evidence. Installing first and applying later means no grant.
  4. Have the charger fitted. Once the claim is approved and the installation is complete, the grant is paid to the installer and comes off what you owe.

Home charger vs the alternatives

An electric car will charge from an ordinary three-pin socket — slowly. Here is how the realistic options compare, and why a dedicated home chargepoint is the one most drivers end up with.

OptionSpeedCost to useGrant helpBest for
Smart 7kW home chargepointAdds roughly 25–30 miles of range per hour; a typical car charges fully overnightLowest — especially on an off-peak tariff (roughly £300 a year for 10,000 miles)Up to £500 for eligible renters, flat owners and on-street householdsMost drivers, most of the time
Three-pin plug (granny cable)Around 2.3kW — roughly 8 miles of range per hourSame home electricity price, but long continuous loads on a household socketNoneOccasional top-ups and emergencies only. Manufacturers advise against using it as your everyday method.
Public rapid chargerVery fast — a large top-up in well under an hour on most carsHighest per kWh of the three optionsNone for individualsLong journeys and drivers with no way to charge at home

In short: a home chargepoint is slower than a public rapid charger but far cheaper to run, and it works while you sleep. If you can fit one — with or without a grant — it is normally the option that makes EV ownership cheapest and simplest.

Buying the car? The Electric Car Grant

Separate from charger help, the Electric Car Grant reduces the price of eligible new electric cars at the dealership — there is nothing to claim back afterwards. Cars in Band 1 get £3,750 off; cars in Band 2 get £1,500 off. Which band a car sits in depends on the government's sustainability assessment of how and where it was built, and only models on the official GOV.UK list qualify. The two grants can sit side by side: £3,750 or £1,500 off the car, and up to £500 off the charger to fuel it at home.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the EV charger grant in 2026?

Up to £500 towards a home chargepoint. It covers 75% of the eligible cost of buying and installing the charger, capped at £500 per socket — so a cheaper installation gets 75% of its cost, not a flat £500.

Who qualifies for the £500 charger grant?

Renters, people who own and live in a flat, and households who park on the street and use an approved cross-pavement charging solution. You need to live at the property, have suitable parking, have any permissions your tenure requires, and use an OZEV-authorised installer.

I own my house and have a driveway. Can I claim?

Not under the current home grant — it is aimed at households the earlier schemes left out. You would pay the full installation cost, typically around £900–£1,300 for a smart 7kW charger, but you still get the low off-peak running costs that make home charging worthwhile.

When does the EV charger grant end?

The chargepoint grant routes — renters and flat owners, on-street, residential landlords and workplaces — are funded until 31 March 2027. Applications need to be made before installation, so leaving it until March 2027 risks missing out if there are delays.

Do I apply for the grant myself?

No. Your OZEV-authorised installer applies on your behalf and claims the money once the work is done. Your job is to provide the evidence they ask for — proof of tenure, vehicle details and any landlord or freeholder permission.

Can renters really get a home charger fitted?

Yes, with your landlord's written permission. Many landlords agree because a chargepoint makes the property more attractive to future tenants — and landlords fitting chargers themselves have their own grant route. Ask before you get quotes, and keep the permission in writing.

What if I have no driveway at all?

Look at cross-pavement charging: a channel set into the pavement lets your cable reach the car safely without trailing across the footpath. It needs your council's approval, and the chargepoint grant can contribute up to £500 (75% of eligible costs) if your arrangement is eligible.

How long does a 7kW home charger take to charge a car?

A 7kW chargepoint adds roughly 25–30 miles of range per hour of charging, so a typical electric car goes from low to full overnight. That is around three times faster than a three-pin plug.

What is the Electric Car Grant, and can I get both grants?

It is a separate discount on the car itself: £3,750 off Band 1 models and £1,500 off Band 2 models on the GOV.UK eligible list, applied at the dealership. Yes, the two can combine — money off an eligible new car, plus up to £500 off your home charger if your housing situation qualifies.

Can a landlord or employer claim instead?

Yes. Residential landlords have a grant route for chargepoints at rental properties (up to £500 per socket), and employers can use the Workplace Charging Scheme for staff and fleet chargers. Both also run until 31 March 2027 and both go through OZEV-authorised installers.
