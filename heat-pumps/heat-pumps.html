The Boiler Upgrade Scheme, in plain English

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) is the main grant for heat pumps in England and Wales. Your installer claims it for you — you do not apply to the government yourself, and the grant comes off the price you are quoted.

SystemGrant
Air source heat pump£7,500
Ground source heat pump£7,500
Air-to-air heat pump£2,500
Biomass boiler£5,000
Eligible off-gas-grid homes replacing oil or LPG — higher uplift£9,000
  • £9,000 uplift: for eligible off-gas-grid homes replacing an oil or LPG system, available until 31 March 2027.
  • 0% VAT: domestic energy-saving installations, including heat pumps, are charged at 0% VAT until March 2027.
  • No EPC requirement: you no longer need an Energy Performance Certificate recommendation for a heat pump — that requirement was scrapped in April 2026.
  • One claim per property: the BUS grant can only be claimed once for a property.
  • MCS-certified installer: the work must be done by an MCS-certified installer, who applies for the grant on your behalf.

What a heat pump actually costs

£12,826

Median cost of an air source heat pump installation, before any grant.

£5,326

Approximate cost after the £7,500 grant is taken off that median price (£12,826 − £7,500).

£895 / yr

Approximate running cost for an air source heat pump at SCOP 3.0, compared with about £1,065 a year for a gas boiler — figures are estimates and depend on your home, tariff and how you use heating.

SCOP is a measure of efficiency over a whole heating season. A SCOP of 3.0 means the heat pump delivers about three units of heat for every unit of electricity it uses. Well-designed systems in well-insulated homes tend to run more cheaply; a survey is what confirms the numbers for your property.

Ground source heat pumps

Ground source systems draw heat from the ground through buried pipework. They usually cost more upfront than air source because of the ground work involved, and they need outdoor space. The two main layouts are:

  • Horizontal loops — pipes buried in trenches across a garden or plot; needs a larger area of land.
  • Vertical boreholes — pipes drilled deep into the ground; needs less surface space but drilling adds cost.

Ground source heat pumps qualify for the same £7,500 BUS grant as air source, and the £9,000 off-gas-grid uplift where eligible.

Savings and timescales

  • Running costs: about £895 a year at SCOP 3.0 versus about £1,065 a year for gas — roughly £170 a year lower on those estimates, before any difference in standing charges or tariffs.
  • Survey to quote: a home survey comes first, so the system is sized for your property rather than guessed.
  • Grant approval: your MCS-certified installer submits the BUS application; installation normally follows once the voucher is in place.
  • Installation: an air source installation is typically a matter of days once booked in; ground source takes longer because of trenches or boreholes.

Exact savings and dates cannot be promised in advance — they depend on your current fuel, insulation, radiators or underfloor heating, and the design your installer proposes.

Funding your heat pump

For most households, funding starts with the grant:

  • £7,500 off an air or ground source heat pump through the Boiler Upgrade Scheme.
  • £9,000 for eligible off-gas-grid homes replacing oil or LPG, until 31 March 2027.
  • £2,500 for an air-to-air heat pump, or £5,000 for a biomass boiler, under the same scheme.
  • 0% VAT on the installation until March 2027, which lowers the total you pay.

Any remaining balance is paid by you — in cash, or on finance or other payment terms if your installer offers them, subject to their check. We do not invent finance rates or providers: ask your installer what they offer before you agree to anything.

How the quote process works

  1. Check your eligibility — answer a few questions about your home and heating in our eligibility checker.
  2. Home survey — an installer assesses your property, heat loss and existing system.
  3. Written quote with the grant deducted — the BUS amount should be shown clearly in the price.
  4. Installer claims the grant — your MCS-certified installer applies on your behalf; there is one claim per property.
  5. Installation and handover — the system is fitted, set up and explained to you.

Quick quiz: is a heat pump right for you?

Ask yourself these three questions:

  1. Is your current boiler old, broken, or oil, LPG or electric? If yes, the case for switching is usually strongest — and off-gas-grid oil or LPG homes may qualify for the £9,000 uplift.
  2. Do you own your home? The BUS grant is for property owners, and it is one claim per property.
  3. Do you have space outside for a unit about the size of an air-conditioning box (air source), or land for loops or boreholes (ground source)?

Answered yes to most of them? Run the full eligibility check to see what you may qualify for.

Frequently asked questions

1. How much is the heat pump grant?

£7,500 for an air source or ground source heat pump through the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, £2,500 for air-to-air, and £5,000 for biomass. Eligible off-gas-grid homes replacing oil or LPG can get £9,000 until 31 March 2027.

2. Do I apply for the grant myself?

No. An MCS-certified installer applies on your behalf, and the grant is deducted from your quote. HeatFund only helps you check whether you may qualify first.

3. Do I need an EPC to get a heat pump grant?

No. The EPC requirement was scrapped in April 2026, so you no longer need an EPC recommendation before applying.

4. Can I claim the grant twice, or on a second property I own?

The rule is one claim per property. A property that has already had a BUS claim cannot claim again under the scheme.

5. How much will a heat pump cost me after the grant?

The median air source installation is £12,826. After the £7,500 grant, that is approximately £5,326. Your own quote will depend on the size of system your home needs and any changes to radiators or pipework.

6. Are heat pumps cheaper to run than a gas boiler?

On the estimates used on this page, about £895 a year at SCOP 3.0 compared with about £1,065 a year for gas. Your actual bills depend on your electricity tariff, insulation and heating habits.
