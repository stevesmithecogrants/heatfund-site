Home battery storage UK 2026: costs, savings & £15k loans

A home battery stores electricity from your solar panels — or cheap off-peak electricity from the grid — so you can use it later, when prices are higher or the sun has gone down. This guide explains sizes, costs, savings, VAT relief and loan help in plain English.

How a home battery works

During the day, solar panels often generate more electricity than a home uses at that moment. Without a battery, the spare electricity is exported to the grid. A battery charges from that spare power first, then discharges in the evening for lighting, appliances, cooking and entertainment.

A battery can also charge from the grid. That only helps financially when you can buy electricity cheaply — for example on a time-of-use tariff with a low overnight rate — and use it when the standard rate is higher.

Most modern home batteries are lithium-based, are controlled by an app, and work automatically. You should not need to switch anything on or off day to day.

Battery sizes and types compared

Battery capacity is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh) — how much energy it can store. Output is measured in kilowatts (kW) — how much power it can deliver at once. The examples below use the size labels in common UK product ranges.

10.75kWh classHomes wanting a step above 10kWh without going to the largest unitsUsable vs total capacity, and compatibility with your inverter

Always compare usable capacity, not just headline capacity, and check the warranty, cycle life and whether backup power during a cut is included or an extra.

What does a home battery cost?

Installed home battery costs in the UK generally fall between £1,500 and £10,000. Small add-on batteries at the lower end, and large or premium systems with backup features at the higher end.

As a midpoint guide, expect around £4,600 for a 5kWh system. Your quote will move up or down based on:

  • Battery capacity and brand
  • Whether a new hybrid inverter is needed
  • Electrical work, cabling and consumer unit changes
  • Backup-power (EPS) features
  • Installation access and location

Get itemised quotes so you can see the battery, inverter, labour and any extras separately.

How much can you save?

Savings come from two places: using more of your own solar electricity instead of exporting it cheaply and buying it back later, and — on a suitable tariff — charging cheaply and discharging at peak times.

A well-used 10kWh battery can save around £343–£640 per year. Homes with larger solar arrays, higher evening use and a time-of-use tariff tend to sit towards the top of that range. Low-use homes sit lower, and payback takes longer.

Do the maths on your own bills: your annual saving multiplied by the warranty period should comfortably beat the installed cost before a battery makes clear financial sense on savings alone. Some households also value backup power and energy independence, which are not captured in a simple payback figure.

Important: a battery can lose money on a flat tariff

If you have no solar and you are on a flat tariff — the same price all day — charging a battery from the grid and using that power later does not save money. You pay for charging losses (no battery is 100% efficient), and there is no price gap to profit from. In that setup a battery can lose money. A battery usually needs solar, a time-of-use tariff, or both, to pay its way.

0% VAT until 31 March 2027

Energy-saving installations in domestic properties benefit from 0% VAT until 31 March 2027, and battery storage falls within that relief when installed in qualifying circumstances. That is a meaningful saving versus the standard 20% VAT rate. See our VAT relief guide for how the relief works, and confirm your installer applies it correctly on your quote.

Warm Homes Loans up to £15,000

Under the Warm Homes Plan, eligible households may be able to access loan help of up to £15,000 towards home energy improvements, which can help spread the cost of measures such as battery storage alongside solar. Loan terms, eligibility and availability can change, so check the current position before you commit and never borrow more than you can afford to repay. Should you get a battery without solar?

Sometimes, yes — if you are on a time-of-use tariff with a genuinely cheap off-peak window, a battery can charge overnight and power your morning and evening use. Without that price gap, and without solar, the case is weak (see the flat tariff warning above).

If you are planning solar soon, installing the battery at the same time as solar panels is often cheaper than two separate jobs, and a hybrid inverter can handle both.

Frequently asked questions

1. How much does a home battery cost in the UK?

Most installed systems cost between £1,500 and £10,000. A 5kWh battery is around £4,600 installed as a guide, while larger systems, premium brands and backup features cost more.

2. How much can a 10kWh battery save?

A well-used 10kWh battery can save roughly £343–£640 a year, depending on your solar generation, evening usage, tariff and how fully the battery cycles each day.

3. What size battery do I need?

Look at your evening and night-time electricity use. Many 2–3 bed homes do well with 5–10kWh. Larger homes or high users may benefit from 10.75kWh, 13.5kWh or similar larger units — but oversizing wastes money if the extra capacity rarely gets used.

4. Can a battery lose money?

Yes. On a flat tariff with no solar, there is no cheap electricity to store, and charging losses mean you get back less energy than you put in. In that situation a battery can lose money.

5. Is VAT charged on home batteries?

Qualifying domestic energy-saving installations benefit from 0% VAT until 31 March 2027. Ask your installer to confirm the VAT treatment in writing on your quote.

6. Can I get a loan to help pay for a battery?

Warm Homes Loan help can be up to £15,000 for eligible households under the Warm Homes Plan. Eligibility, terms and availability can change, so check the current details before applying.

7. Do I need solar panels for a battery to work?

No — a battery can charge from the grid. But it usually only saves money from grid charging on a time-of-use tariff with cheap off-peak rates. With solar, savings are usually clearer.

8. Will a battery power my home in a power cut?

Only if the system includes backup (EPS) functionality and is wired for it. Many standard systems switch off during a cut for safety. Ask specifically if backup matters to you.

9. How long does a home battery last?

Check the manufacturer's warranty and cycle rating for the exact model you are quoted. Capacity fades gradually over time, so compare warranties side by side when comparing quotes.

10. Is a battery worth it for me?

It depends on your solar, tariff and evening use. Add up your likely yearly saving and compare it honestly with the installed cost. Check your eligibility and we will talk through the options for your home — with no grant promised before a check.
