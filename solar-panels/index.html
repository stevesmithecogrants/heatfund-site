Solar panels UK 2026: costs, savings & free grants

Solar panels let your home make its own electricity from daylight. This guide explains, in plain English, what a system costs in 2026, what you might save, how the 0% VAT rate works, and the honest checks to do before you sign anything.

How solar panels work

Panels on your roof turn daylight — not just direct sun — into electricity for your home. You use that electricity first, which means you buy less from your supplier. Any you do not use can be stored in a battery for the evening, or exported to the grid if you have an export arrangement in place.

A standard home system is usually 3kW to 6kW. Most family homes quoted in 2026 are around 4kW, which is roughly 10 panels, depending on the panel size and the roof space available.

Three things worth understanding

Solar Together & Hub Saver schemes

Some councils run group-buying schemes — often called Solar Together — where households in an area buy together so installers can offer a keener price than a one-off job. A “Hub Saver” style offer works in a similar way: grouping demand in one area to cut scaffolding, survey and admin costs.

These schemes can help, but they are not a grant and they are not available everywhere. Always compare the group price with at least one independent quote, check which panels and inverter are included, and confirm the installer is MCS-certified.

MCS certification

MCS is the UK certification scheme for small-scale renewable installations. Using an MCS-certified installer matters: it is the standard lenders, insurers and export tariff providers look for, and it is normally required if you want to be paid for electricity you export.

Ask for the installer’s MCS certificate number and check it before you pay a deposit.

0% VAT until 31 March 2027: Domestic installations of energy-saving materials, including solar panels, are currently charged VAT at 0% in Great Britain until 31 March 2027. That saving is built into a proper installer quote — you should not be charged 20% VAT on top for a qualifying domestic installation in that period. Always check the VAT line on your written quote.

Installer costs and what quotes cover

A proper written quote should be itemised. If a quote is just one total figure, ask for the breakdown before you compare it with anyone else.

  • Panels, inverter and mounting kit, named by brand and model
  • Scaffolding and roof work
  • Electrical work, consumer unit changes if needed, and certification
  • MCS certification and registration paperwork
  • VAT shown clearly — 0% for a qualifying domestic installation until 31 March 2027
  • Warranties: panel performance, inverter, and workmanship, each stated separately
  • What is not included: battery, bird proofing, roof repairs, or a new roof

Cheapest is not always best value. A slightly higher quote from an MCS-certified installer with a clear warranty and a local contact can be worth more than a low headline price with extras added later.

Can solar cut your bills before 2027?

Yes — savings start from the day the system is switched on, because every unit you generate and use is a unit you do not buy. The 31 March 2027 date matters for a different reason: it is when the current 0% VAT rate for domestic energy-saving installations is due to end. Installing before that date can keep the VAT saving that is built into today’s quotes.

There is no need to rush into a bad deal to beat a date. Get the survey, check the roof, compare itemised quotes, and only proceed when the numbers work for your home.

Frequently asked questions

Do solar panels work on cloudy days?

Yes. They generate less than on a bright day, but they still produce electricity from daylight. Annual figures matter more than any single day.

Do I need planning permission?

Most home rooftop systems are permitted development, but flats, listed buildings and conservation areas can be different. Your installer should confirm this for your property before work starts.

Is there a free grant for solar panels?

There is no single universal free solar grant for every household. Help depends on the scheme, your area, your income or benefits, and your property. Some households may qualify through wider home-upgrade schemes — check the current routes in our Government Grants guide and start with a free eligibility check.

What does 0% VAT actually save me?

For a qualifying domestic installation, VAT is charged at 0% instead of the standard rate until 31 March 2027. On a £6,500 system that is a material difference versus a 20% rate — check the VAT line is shown as 0% on your quote. See our VAT Relief guide.

Should I add a battery at the same time?

Not always. A battery can help you use more of your own solar in the evening, but it adds cost. Many households start with panels, watch their usage for a few months, then decide. Our Battery Storage guide explains the trade-off.

How long do panels last?

Panels are built for long service and usually come with long performance warranties — ask for the exact terms in writing. Inverters typically have shorter warranties than panels, so check both.
