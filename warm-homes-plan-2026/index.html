What replaces ECO4? Warm Homes Plan 2026 explained

Last updated: 7 November 2026 · Written by the HeatFund editorial team · Checked against GOV.UK guidance

ECO4 is due to end on 31 December 2026. From January 2027, support for low-income homes in England moves under the Warm Homes Plan — with the Warm Homes: Local Grant already running through councils as the main route you can apply for today.

Key Takeaways

  • The Warm Homes: Local Grant is live now: £500 million for England, running April 2025 to March 2028, delivered by local councils.
  • You may qualify with household income of £36,000 or less, a qualifying benefit, or a home in a low-income postcode area — and your home must be EPC D–G.
  • For eligible owner-occupiers, agreed measures are fully funded. Landlords get one home fully funded, then contribute 50% for further homes.
  • The wider Warm Homes Plan is capped at £13 billion in total, focused first on low-income households, social housing and the worst-performing homes.
  • ECO4 continues until 31 December 2026. From January 2027 there is no like-for-like ECO4 replacement — the Warm Homes routes take over in England.

Warm Homes: Local Grant — the live scheme

This is the part of the Warm Homes Plan most households will use first. Funding is allocated to councils in England, and councils arrange the survey, agree the measures and appoint the installer. You apply through the GOV.UK service or directly with your council — you do not choose your own installer for funded work, and you should never pay a third party upfront to “secure” it.

Measures are survey-led: insulation first where the home needs it, then low-carbon heating such as a heat pump, plus heating controls and solar where they suit the property. Not every council area is open at all times — some pause when their allocation is full — so a postcode check is the right first step.

Warm Homes Plan 2027: the forward plan

The Warm Homes Plan is the government’s longer programme to upgrade cold, inefficient homes and cut energy bills, with total funding capped at £13 billion. Its priorities are clear: low-income households first, homes with the lowest EPC ratings, and rented and social homes that are hardest to heat.

Main workstreams

  • Local Grant delivery — council-led upgrades for low-income private homes in England, as above.
  • Social housing upgrades — funding for social landlords to improve EPC D–G homes.
  • Private rented homes — support tied to minimum energy efficiency standards, with the landlord contribution rules above.
  • Low-carbon heat — heat pumps and heat networks alongside the separate Boiler Upgrade Scheme, which continues in its own right.

Delivery stays local: councils know their housing stock, verify eligibility, and manage installers. That is why availability, waiting times and the exact package differ by area — there is no single national installer or single application form beyond the GOV.UK entry point.

What replaces ECO4 from January 2027?

Nothing replaces ECO4 one-for-one. ECO4 is an obligation on energy suppliers; the Warm Homes Plan is government-funded and council-delivered. In practice, from January 2027:

  • In England, low-income households should look first to the Warm Homes: Local Grant and their council’s Warm Homes routes.
  • The Boiler Upgrade Scheme remains the route for a heat pump grant contribution if you do not qualify for fully funded help.
  • 0% VAT on domestic energy-saving installations continues to March 2027 under current rules.
  • Scotland and Wales run their own schemes — Warmer Homes Scotland and Nest — and are not covered by the Local Grant.

If you receive a qualifying benefit and your home is EPC D–G, do not wait for 2027: check ECO4 while it is still open, and check the Local Grant in parallel. You can read our separate guides to the ECO4 scheme and government grants for the full picture.

How to apply

  • Run a free check on HeatFund at our eligibility checker, or apply directly through the GOV.UK Warm Homes: Local Grant service.
  • Have your EPC rating ready if you know it — if not, a check will look it up from your postcode and address.
  • Expect contact from your council within 10 working days, then a home survey before any measures are agreed.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Warm Homes: Local Grant really free?

For eligible owner-occupiers, yes — agreed measures are fully funded and you pay nothing. Tenants pay nothing. Landlords get one property fully funded and then contribute 50% towards further properties.

My income is over £36,000 — can I still qualify?

Possibly. The income route is only one of three: you may also qualify through a means-tested benefit received in the household, or because your home is in an eligible low-income postcode area. Your home still needs to be EPC D–G.

Does ECO4 end in December 2026?

Yes, ECO4 is scheduled to run to 31 December 2026. Work that needs ECO4 funding should be completed within the scheme rules and deadlines, so early checks matter.

What replaces ECO4 in 2027?

There is no direct ECO4 clone. In England the Warm Homes Plan — led by the Local Grant and council delivery — becomes the main funded route for low-income homes from January 2027.

Can I pick my own installer?

No. Under the Local Grant your council appoints installers for funded work. Be wary of anyone asking for an upfront fee to access this grant.

How long until I hear back?

Councils will usually contact applicants within 10 working days of an application through GOV.UK. Survey and installation dates then depend on local capacity.

Is this available in Scotland or Wales?

No — the Local Grant is England only. Scotland has Warmer Homes Scotland and Wales has Nest.
