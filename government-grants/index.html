Government energy grants UK 2026: every current scheme

There is no single “energy grant”. Help comes from several separate schemes, each with its own rules, amounts and closing dates. This page puts them all in one place, in plain English, so you can see which ones may fit your home before you apply anywhere.

HeatFund is an independent eligibility-check service. We are not the government, we do not administer these grants, and we do not install anything. Nothing on this page is a promise of funding — every grant depends on an eligibility check.

How government energy grants work

Most schemes pay towards the cost of improving a home’s heating or energy efficiency — they rarely hand cash to the householder. In most cases the money goes to an approved installer or to your local council’s delivery partner once the work is agreed.

Who sets the rules?

Each scheme is set by the UK Government, the Scottish or Welsh Government, or your local council. They decide the amounts, who qualifies, and when the scheme closes.

Who gets the money?

Usually the installer, council or energy supplier delivering the work — not you directly. The Warm Home Discount is the exception: it is taken off your electricity bill by your supplier.

Can schemes be combined?

Sometimes. For example, 0% VAT relief can apply alongside a grant-funded installation, but the same piece of work cannot normally be funded twice. Always check before you commit.

Watch the closing dates. ECO4 is funded to 31 December 2026, while the Warm Homes: Local Grant runs to March 2028. Schemes can also pause or change when funding is allocated, so checking sooner rather than later is sensible.

Getting a grant in 3 steps

1

Check your eligibility

Answer a few questions about your property, how you heat it, your EPC rating if you know it, and your circumstances. That points you to the scheme or schemes that may fit.

2

Home assessment

Most funded work starts with a survey of your home. The assessor confirms what is suitable — for example whether a heat pump would work well in your property — and what funding can cover.

3

Work carried out

If you qualify and agree to go ahead, an approved installer does the work and the grant is claimed as part of that process. You should never be asked to pay a grant fee upfront.

Government grant questions

Can I apply for more than one grant?You can often be considered for more than one scheme, but the same work cannot normally be paid for twice. A check will show which scheme fits best, and whether 0% VAT relief also applies to the work.

Do I have to be on benefits to get a grant?No. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme and VAT relief do not require benefits. ECO4, the Warm Homes: Local Grant and Nest are aimed mainly at lower-income households, where benefits or a low household income are common routes in.

Do landlords qualify?In some cases, yes — ECO4 and local Warm Homes funding can cover rented homes with the landlord’s permission, and landlords can use the Boiler Upgrade Scheme for rental properties they own, subject to the scheme rules.

Is the money paid to me?Usually not. For installations, the funding is normally paid to the installer, council or supplier delivering the work. The Warm Home Discount is different — your supplier takes £150 off your electricity bill.

When do these schemes end?ECO4 is funded to 31 December 2026 and the Warm Homes: Local Grant runs to March 2028. Other schemes are reviewed by the government and can change, so figures here reflect the published position for 2026.

Does checking cost anything, and does it guarantee a grant?The HeatFund eligibility check is free, and it never guarantees a grant. It shows which schemes you may qualify for; the final decision always rests with the scheme, council or supplier running the assessment.
