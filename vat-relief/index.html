0% VAT on solar panels, heat pumps & battery storage

If you are improving the energy efficiency of your home, you may not have to pay VAT on the installation at all. A temporary zero rate applies to domestic energy-saving installations across Great Britain — and it can take hundreds or even thousands of pounds off a quote.

Key takeaways

  • 0% VAT applies to the installation of energy-saving materials in domestic properties in Great Britain until 31 March 2027.
  • From 1 April 2027 the rate is scheduled to step back up to the reduced rate of 5% — still well below the standard 20%.
  • The relief covers the installation work and the materials supplied as part of that same installation — for example solar panels, heat pumps and battery storage fitted by your installer.
  • You do not claim it back afterwards. Your installer should charge 0% VAT on the invoice for qualifying work, so check the quote before the job starts.
  • VAT relief sits alongside grants, not instead of them — it can apply on top of help such as the Boiler Upgrade Scheme and ECO4 where you qualify.

How the zero rate works

VAT is normally added to home improvement work at the standard rate. For a set list of energy-saving installations, the government cut that rate first to a reduced rate and then, from April 2022, to zero for homes in Great Britain. The idea is simple: lowering the tax on the work itself makes it cheaper for households to cut their energy use.

The relief is given at the point of sale. There is no form to send to HMRC and no refund to wait for — if the work qualifies, the installer charges no VAT on it in the first place. That is why the wording on your quote matters: if VAT has been added at 20% to a job that should be zero-rated, raise it with the installer before you agree to anything.

The property must be used for domestic purposes, and the relief is for the installation service together with the materials the installer supplies as part of that job. Buying equipment on its own, without installation, is not covered in the same way.

Which installations qualify?

Generally covered

  • Solar panels, installed on or near your home
  • Heat pumps — air source, ground source and water source
  • Battery storage for a domestic energy system
  • Insulation for walls, roofs, floors and hot water systems
  • Draught stripping and draught-proofing measures
  • Heating controls supplied and fitted with qualifying work
  • Other energy-saving materials on HMRC's published list

Generally not covered

  • Equipment bought separately with no installation service
  • Work on a property that is not used for domestic purposes
  • General building work that is not itself an energy-saving installation
  • Fossil fuel boilers — a standard replacement gas boiler is not an energy-saving installation for this relief

Mixed jobs can be split: the qualifying part at 0% and the rest at the normal rate. Ask for the VAT treatment to be shown clearly on your written quote.

VAT relief and grants together

The zero rate is not a grant, and you do not need a grant to get it — any household having qualifying work installed benefits automatically. Where you also qualify for grant funding, the two can work side by side: a grant such as the Boiler Upgrade Scheme can reduce the cost of a heat pump, and the 0% rate means no VAT is added to the installation on top of that.

Grant eligibility is a separate check, based on your home, your heating system and, for some schemes, your income or benefits. You can read more in our government grants guide, or go straight to the eligibility checker to see what may be open to you.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to apply for the 0% VAT rate?

No. There is no application and nothing to claim back. If the work qualifies, your installer applies the zero rate on the invoice. Your job is simply to check the quote shows 0% VAT for the qualifying installation before the work begins.

When does the 0% rate end?

The zero rate for domestic energy-saving installations in Great Britain runs until 31 March 2027. From 1 April 2027 those installations are scheduled to move to the reduced VAT rate of 5%, which is still lower than the standard 20% charged on most building work.

Does battery storage qualify on its own?

Yes. Battery storage installed in a domestic property is on the list of energy-saving installations, so it can be zero-rated whether it is fitted alongside new solar panels or added to a system you already have — as long as it is supplied and installed as part of the job.

What if I buy the panels or battery myself and pay someone to fit them?

That usually changes the VAT treatment. The zero rate covers materials an installer supplies as part of the installation service. Equipment you buy separately, without installation, is normally charged at the standard 20% rate, and a labour-only fitting may not bring the equipment into the relief.

Does the relief apply to a new gas boiler?

No. A replacement fossil fuel boiler is not classed as an energy-saving installation for this relief, so the normal VAT rules apply. The zero rate is aimed at low-carbon and energy-saving measures such as heat pumps, solar and insulation instead.

Can I get VAT relief and a grant for the same job?

In many cases, yes. They are separate forms of support: a grant helps fund the cost, and the zero VAT rate means no tax is added to the qualifying installation. What you can combine depends on the scheme, so have your eligibility checked before you rely on either.

My installer has charged 20% VAT — what should I do?

Raise it before you pay, not after. Ask the installer to confirm in writing which rate applies and why. If the whole job was a qualifying energy-saving installation in a domestic property, it should normally have been zero-rated, and correcting an invoice is far easier before the work is finished and paid for.

Does 0% VAT apply everywhere in the UK?

The zero rate described on this page applies to domestic installations in Great Britain — England, Scotland and Wales. Northern Ireland has its own arrangements for energy-saving installations, so the saving there can differ.
